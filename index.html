A. Definition and Types of Narrative Text

Definition of Narrative Text

Narrative text is a text genre in English that presents a series of events arranged chronologically and connected by cause and effect, involving characters who experience a problem or conflict (complication) until the problem is eventually resolved (resolution).

"A narrative text is not simply a report of what happened — it is a story built around a problem and its resolution." (Anderson & Anderson, 2003)

Socially, narrative text fulfills four primary functions in communication and cultural preservation:

1. To Entertain (Menghibur)

Engages readers through suspense, conflict, and a satisfying resolution—such as Princess Kilisuci's impossible well-digging challenge to Lembu Suro and the fierce battle between Sura and Baya.

2. To Inform (Menginformasikan)

Conveys local wisdom and historical/geographical origins—such as explaining the volcanic eruptions of Mount Kelud and the naming of Surabaya.

3. To Teach or Persuade (Menanamkan Nilai)

Instills moral and character values: Lembu Suro's downfall warns against arrogance, while Sura and Baya's clash teaches the importance of keeping mutual agreements.

4. To Preserve Culture (Melestarikan Budaya)

Keeps East Java's folktales and regional identity alive and meaningful for Generation Z learners.

1. Orientation Introduces characters (who), place (where), and time (when).
2. Complication Raises the central conflict, crisis, or unexpected problem.
3. Resolution Resolves the conflict (either happy or tragic ending).
4. Re-orientation / Coda Optional closing moral lesson or character transformation.
Model 1: The Legend of Mount Kelud (Lembu Suro)

Orientation: In Kediri lived Princess Dyah Ayu Kilisuci, courted by a powerful but arrogant giant named Lembu Suro.

Complication: To refuse him politely, Kilisuci challenged him to dig a deep well in one night; he nearly succeeded.

Resolution: Fearing his success, the princess and her people buried him inside the well with stones and soil; before dying, he cursed the land to erupt.

Coda: Teaches that excessive arrogance and ambition bring destruction.

Model 2: The Legend of Suro and Boyo (Surabaya)

Orientation: Long ago in the Strait of Madura lived Sura (a shark) and Baya (a crocodile), rulers of sea and river.

Complication: After agreeing to divide sea and land territories, Sura broke the agreement by hunting in the river.

Resolution: A fierce battle broke out until both were exhausted and wounded.

Coda: The area was named "Surabaya", reminding us to respect agreed boundaries and promises.

Model 3: The Origin of Banyuwangi (Sri Tanjung)

Orientation: In Blambangan lived faithful Sri Tanjung and her nobleman husband Sidapaksa.

Complication: Blinded by a false rumor spread during his absence, Sidapaksa refused to believe Sri Tanjung's innocence.

Resolution: She asked to be thrown into the river; when her body touched the water, it turned sweet and fragrant.

Coda: Named Banyuwangi (fragrant water), warning against judging without seeking the truth.

The Legend of Mount Kelud (Annotated)

Orientation Long ago, in the kingdom of Kediri, there lived a beautiful princess named Dyah Ayu Kilisuci. She was known throughout the land for her wisdom and kindness.

Complication Among her suitors was a giant named Lembu Suro, who possessed extraordinary strength but was arrogant. To refuse him politely, she gave him two conditions: dig a deep well in one night and enter it to prove its depth.

Resolution Working through the night, he dug an enormous well before dawn. As soon as he climbed down, the princess and her people poured soil and stones into the well, burying him alive. Realizing he had been tricked, Lembu Suro cursed the land to erupt into fire and disaster.

Coda People believe his curse is connected to Mount Kelud's eruptions, teaching that unchecked pride brings destruction.

The Legend of Sura and Baya (Annotated)

Orientation Long ago, in the strait between Madura and East Java, there lived a shark named Sura and a crocodile named Baya, both proud of their strength.

Complication One day, they quarreled over hunting grounds and agreed Sura would rule the sea while Baya ruled the land and rivers. However, Sura grew hungry and secretly entered the river, breaking their agreement.

Resolution Furious at the betrayal, Baya confronted Sura. They fought fiercely where river met sea until both were exhausted and wounded.

Coda The place was named "Surabaya", reminding us that breaking promises harms everyone involved.

Reading Passage: The Legend of Sri Tanjung (Origin of Banyuwangi)

A long time ago, in the kingdom of Blambangan, there lived a beautiful princess named Sri Tanjung, happily married to a brave young nobleman named Sidapaksa. One day, the king ordered Sidapaksa to travel to a distant kingdom. During his absence, a wicked minister spread a false rumour that Sri Tanjung had been unfaithful. When Sidapaksa finally returned, he was furious and refused to believe her explanation.

Deeply hurt, Sri Tanjung asked Sidapaksa to throw her body into the river, saying the water would turn fragrant if she had been faithful. As soon as her body touched the water, the river suddenly released a sweet fragrance. Realizing his terrible mistake, Sidapaksa was overwhelmed with regret, and the fragrant river was named Banyuwangi (from banyu = water and wangi = fragrant).
